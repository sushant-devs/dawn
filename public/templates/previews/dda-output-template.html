Advancing First-Line Treatment in HR+/HER2- Metastatic Breast Cancer

Patients with HR+/HER2- metastatic breast cancer face limited options with endocrine therapy alone — with disease progression and resistance driving the need for more effective targeted combinations. BREXIVA® offers a once-daily oral CDK4/6 inhibitor that significantly extends progression-free survival.

Important Safety Information

Full ISI — Please Read
⚠ Warnings & Precautions

Neutropenia: Neutropenia is the most frequently reported adverse reaction. Monitor complete blood counts prior to start, at the beginning of each cycle, on Day 15 of the first 2 cycles, and as clinically indicated. Dose interruption, reduction, or discontinuation may be required.

Hepatotoxicity: Increases in ALT and AST have been observed. Monitor liver function tests prior to start and as clinically indicated. Grade ≥3 elevations may require dose interruption, reduction, or discontinuation.

Contraindications

No contraindications are identified in the current U.S. Prescribing Information.

Adverse Reactions

The most common adverse reactions (≥5%) in clinical trials were:

  • Neutropenia (monitor CBC regularly)
  • Nausea
  • Diarrhea
  • Fatigue
  • Alopecia
Drug Interactions

Strong CYP3A inhibitors may increase Brexiva exposure; avoid concomitant use or reduce Brexiva dose to 75 mg. Strong CYP3A inducers may decrease Brexiva exposure; avoid concomitant use.

Use in Specific Populations
  • Pregnancy: Can cause fetal harm. Advise females of reproductive potential to use effective contraception during treatment and for at least 3 weeks after the last dose.
  • Lactation: Advise not to breastfeed during treatment and for at least 3 weeks after the last dose.
  • Pediatric Use: Safety and effectiveness not established in pediatric patients.
  • Hepatic Impairment: Reduce dose to 75 mg once daily for moderate hepatic impairment. Avoid use in severe hepatic impairment.
Indication

BREXIVA® (tablets for oral use) is indicated for the treatment of HR+/HER2- metastatic breast cancer in combination with an aromatase inhibitor as initial endocrine-based therapy in postmenopausal women.
